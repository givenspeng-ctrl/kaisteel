A catalogue shows what a supplier wants to sell. Due diligence establishes who will contract, what will actually be supplied, which evidence applies and how non-conforming goods are controlled.

1. Legal and commercial identity

  • Legal entity, jurisdiction and registration number.
  • Registered address and contracting bank-account identity.
  • Quotation validity, Incoterm and named place or port.
  • Warranty issuer and claims contact.

2. Product definition

  • Revision-controlled drawings and final dimensions.
  • Approved BOM, finishes and appliance models.
  • Electrical, gas, water and drainage interfaces.
  • Clear exclusions and local-installation responsibilities.

3. Evidence matching

  • Material evidence linked to the selected grade and batch — see what material documents to request.
  • Tests matched to the actual substrate and coating system.
  • Certificates matched to exact model, ratings and market — see verifying an appliance certificate.
  • No component certificate presented as whole-system certification.

4. Quality and shipment

  • Defined inspection points and acceptance criteria.
  • HOLD, corrective action and reinspection rules.
  • Packing BOM, protection method and loading plan.
  • Order-specific document index before release.
KAISTEEL control: required evidence is defined at Technical Freeze, collected during production and indexed in the order-specific Compliance Dossier before shipment release.
